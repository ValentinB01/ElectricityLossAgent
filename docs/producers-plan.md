## What each step must not forget

- **Step 2:** the healthy filter keeps 97% of rows and must not use the setpoint. Validation also chose the inputs and the number of trees, so its score is slightly optimistic. The 2020 score is still to come: `--eval --final`, once, with settings frozen.
- **Step 3:** the check was changed after the first run, with Valentin, from "fewer false alarms than the fixed threshold" (which almost never fires) to a target of 0.1 per turbine-month. The filter and the icing rule were also added after that first look at validation, so 2020 is the real proof. Icing is untested: Nov–Dec 2019 had no frost. Real faults are messier than injected ones, which have a constant effect. Loss estimates are off by 0.22 MWh/day on a true 0.70 on average.
- **Step 4:** M1-W's hourly error (9.3% of rated) comes from the weather data, not the model: air density, gusts and temperature barely beat wind alone. Its value is a calibrated band (79% coverage) for `cost_mwh_p90`. At Fântânele it uses a Senvion MM92 curve on an assumed 100 m hub, and one forecast point serves every turbine. Safety limits and the crew shift are assumptions at the top of `downtime_cost.py`.
- **Step 5:** assumptions to confirm with PPC: crews work every day including weekends; a job split over two days restarts the turbine overnight; a repair bundled into a revision fits inside the revision's stop and crew time. Real `Scheduled Maintenance` is about 20 h per turbine per year against our 40 h. With only six turbines, the fleet comparison breaks when half are faulted at once: the demo's three faults raised one spurious sensor check. M2 now rules out loss-only causes for a turbine producing above expected, which cut its validation false alarms to 0.01 per turbine-month.
- **Step 6:** settings were frozen before the single 2020 run. Test year: M1 1.88% vs. 2.24% error, coverage 79%; M1-W ties its baseline again; M2 detects as many faults as the fixed threshold (62.9% vs. 63.2%) with 30 times fewer false alarms (0.024 vs. 0.735), so it misses the step 3 check as worded. M2 is weak on yaw errors (39%), icing (15%) and losses under 2% (23%). In the backtest the agent wins in every fault set and both forecasts. Most of the gain is moving revisions to calm days (54 of 57 MWh with a perfect forecast); for repairs, lower downtime (−29 MWh) is nearly cancelled by longer waits (+26 MWh). The noise model (3% per day ahead) is mild: no archived forecasts exist for 2020 to measure real forecast error.
- **Step 7:** done, indicative only (34 days, one season, no location, seeded rain). Plant 1's DC column is a unit error, so AC only; Plant 2 has real outages and weak inverters, so M2-PV measures noise robustly and sets its false-alarm rate on inverters that look healthy. On the last 7 days M2-PV detects 75% vs. 83% for the fixed threshold, with 4 times fewer false alarms; soiling (losses under 2%) is its blind spot, and its cause F1 fell to 0.64. Most of its 'false' alarms are on inverters with real problems. After the 2020 backtest, short jobs were kept to one day (they had been split overnight to catch cheap hours); on the dev period this lowers the agent's loss and raises the simple policies', so 2020 was not flattered. No public SCADA exists for PPC's parks: demo findings are replayed and labelled as such.
